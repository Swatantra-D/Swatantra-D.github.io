From the bench to brain dynamics

I came to brain science the long way round. I started in biotechnology, with a BSc (Hons) at Shoolini University and a bachelor's thesis on telomeres and ageing in yeast, then moved into medical biotechnology and bioinformatics for my MSc at JIS University, Kolkata, where I graduated first in my class. Projects in deep learning and neuroinformatics kept pulling me towards computation and the brain, and in 2024 I joined the Center for Brain Science and Applications at IIT Jodhpur for an MS by Research in Artificial Intelligence and Data Science, which I completed in 2026.

Today I am a Senior Project Associate in Dr. Dipanjan Roy's Cognitive Brain Dynamics Lab (IIT Jodhpur and the National Brain Research Center), studying how large-scale brain dynamics change as we age. My MS thesis characterised an age-associated slowing in how fast the brain transitions between states, using fMRI recorded during movie watching, rest and a sensorimotor task. I now relate functional dynamics and network structure to healthy ageing. Next, I am looking for position in Neurotech and NeuroAI where I can bridge the gap between biological and artificial learnings.

My training spans three quite different benches. In the wet lab I have worked with PCR, gel electrophoresis, SDS-PAGE and cell culture. In deep learning, I fitted autoencoders and CNNs to segment skin layers in optical coherence tomography scans of healing wounds at IIT Kharagpur, work related to an IEEE EMBC 2023 paper, and trained neural networks and random forests to predict enzyme classes from peptide statistics. In neuroscience, I wrote MATLAB tools for sharp-wave-ripple and non-REM sleep analysis at the Donders Institute, and I now work on fMRI preprocessing, denoising and time-series modelling, with side quests into EEG with OpenBCI and Arduino-based biosignal recording.

Outside the lab, I founded IIT Jodhpur's NeuroTech Society and ran two editions of its BCI hackathon, and I teach whenever I can, including as an invited lecturer for the International Youth Neuroscience Association. Away from screens you will find me cycling, running or playing badminton, on the harmonica or the keyboard, gardening, cooking, reading, taking photographs, and generally trying to find patterns in the universe.
