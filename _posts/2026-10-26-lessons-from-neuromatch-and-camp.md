In 2021 I took two Neuromatch Academy courses back to back: Computational Neuroscience (30 days) and Deep Learning (21 days). Two years later I spent sixteen days at the Computational Approaches to Memory and Plasticity (CAMP) workshop at IISER Pune, supported by the Simons Foundation. Coming from biotechnology, these were intense, concentrated stretches of learning. Here is what stuck.

<!--more-->

## 1. Scope the question until it fits

My Neuromatch projects had short, concrete titles: *Predicting image identities from neural activities* and *Predicting visual fMRI responses to videos with a neuro-inspired network*. A project with a deadline forces you to cut a big curiosity down to one answerable question, with data you can actually get. Writing the question as a single sentence before starting is a habit worth keeping.

## 2. Implementing something is how you understand it

Reading about a model and writing the twenty lines that simulate it are different kinds of knowing. The tutorials have you build things from scratch before reaching for a library. When a method is confusing, coding a toy version of it is a good first move.

## 3. Learn alongside other people

Neuromatch puts students into small “pods” with a teaching assistant, mixing people from different fields. Explaining an idea to someone from a different background, whether physics, psychology or engineering, is the fastest way to discover that you do not quite understand it yet. It also made the field feel far less intimidating.

## 4. Breadth first, then depth

CAMP sat between computation and the biology of memory and plasticity, which suited someone who came from a wet lab. Seeing how theorists and experimentalists frame the same question differently made me more careful about which level of description a claim is actually about.

## 5. Give back

Later I volunteered with Neuromatch: analysing academy feedback, mediating issues on Discord, and helping with social events, outreach and conference planning. Seeing a course from the organisers' side shows how much invisible work goes into making a learning community feel welcoming.

## If you are thinking of applying

Go. Pick the course slightly outside your comfort zone, be honest in your group about what you do not know, and treat the project as practice in asking good questions rather than as a race for a result.
